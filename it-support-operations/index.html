What this data can and cannot tell you

Reliable: every ticket created, its title, requester, and timestamp. Zendesk notifies the Information Technology group on every assignment, so ticket creation is captured completely. Weekly volume derived here matches the independent #alerts-it-tickets Slack feed to within one or two per week across twelve weeks.

Not available: Zendesk does not send solved or closed notifications to your inbox, so open state and true backlog aging cannot be derived from email. Agent replies only route to you on tickets that came through hr-it@ and it-support@govspend.com, which is 48 of 236. The standups named the problem as IT acting as a switchboard. The data says something more specific: roughly half the queue is structured process work wearing a support ticket costume, and it is concentrated in a handful of requesters. Migrating that to JSM unchanged just moves it. The sequence that matters is form first, then queue, then tool.

Recommended before the migration

  1. Build the HR lifecycle form, not a ticket type

    Onboarding, offboarding, promotion, transfer, leave, return. Six structured intakes with required fields and a start date. This alone addresses 55 tickets a quarter and removes the eight-day tail, because a form with a date can drive an SLA and a ticket titled "New Hire EE Code" cannot.

  2. Give distribution lists an owner other than IT

    42 tickets of email plumbing. Group ownership delegated to team leads in Google Workspace removes most of it outright. What stays becomes a two-field request.

  3. Turn phishing reports into a button

    19 tickets are users forwarding suspicious mail to ask if it is real. That is good user behavior landing in the wrong system. With KnowBe4 already deployed, the report button routes it to security instead of the support queue.

  4. Instrument three fields before you migrate, not after

    Request versus incident. Resolved in house versus escalated. Time to first response. Without these, the JSM business case is an assertion, and the queue review you want to run weekly has nothing to review.

  5. Pull the CSV export and close the gaps in this analysis

    Resolution time, backlog age, reopen rate, and who actually closed what are all invisible from email. It is one export, and Jeremy floated it himself on July 22.

Two things that need a decision, not a ticket

Your own domain is the lure

77 messages arrived claiming to be from govspend.com. 51 failed DMARC and 26 had no DMARC record to check. 28 of them reached an inbox — more than half of all inbox leakage in the entire dataset.

The subjects are procurement-shaped and aimed at a procurement company: "Fahey and Sons Signed Agreement" (28 messages) and "RVSA Contract #211 Bid Clarification" (20). A DMARC policy of p=reject ends this category outright. Failing DMARC and still being delivered means the policy is currently set to none or monitor.

Fedmine is being quarantined wholesale

72 messages from fedmine.us, every one with the identical subject "Notice of Bid Protest Filing at the GAO," every one quarantined, every one labelled business email compromise.

Fedmine is a live GovSpend integration — ticket #7073 was a Fedmine link error on the portal this month — and a user has already marked one of these as not phish. Either a partner feed is compromised, or Defend is eating 72 legitimate GAO bid protest notices for a company whose business is procurement intelligence. Both readings are urgent and they need opposite responses.

What to do with this

  1. Call Adam Fehd today

    On Aug 25 at 22:56 a business email compromise message titled "Package Delivery" reached his inbox and the log records a reply. No link was clicked, so this is very likely recoverable, but a reply confirms a live mailbox to the sender and usually starts a conversation rather than ending one. This is the only response in 631 messages.

  2. Set DMARC to reject

    The single highest-leverage change available. It removes 77 messages, 28 of them inbox-delivered, and it costs nothing but the alignment work. Everything else on this page is detection; this is prevention.

  3. Decide on Fedmine this week

    Check whether the GAO notices are genuine, then either allow-list the feed or treat a partner integration as compromised. Leaving 72 quarantined messages undiagnosed is the worst of the three options.

  4. Brief the Haskins campaign targets by name

    Lisa Fisher, Jacob Carrillo and Andrew Ortis have absorbed 72 of the 101 messages between them. A four-week impersonation campaign against named people warrants a direct conversation, not a quarterly training module. Tell Nate too — his name is the one being used.

  5. Reprioritize the migration queue by threat volume

    The fleet tab has 80 people left to enroll and no stated ordering. This dataset supplies one. Marc DiGeronimo, Shea Meadow, Ronald Bjornsson, Geovanny Fajardo and Jacob Carrillo are the five most-targeted people with neither agent installed.

  6. Keep Defend

    631 to 2 is not a close call, and the POC produced a real campaign discovery in its first month. The open question is tuning, not whether it earns its place.
